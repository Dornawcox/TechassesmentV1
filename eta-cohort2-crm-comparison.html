Why this cohort exists

Community-based agricultural organizations — farmer unions, food policy councils, regional commodity associations, agroecology networks, watershed councils — face a recurring decision point: their incumbent CRM is operationally painful, a polished commercial alternative is on offer, and both are hosted on the same handful of US hyperscale clouds. The board or steering committee is asked to vote on the upgrade. The vote frequently happens before the data-sovereignty implications have been mapped.

This cohort exists to make that mapping available before the vote. Ten platforms are scored under the same ETA v1.0 framework that produced Cohort 1's community-infrastructure assessment (Signal, Matrix, Discourse, Hylo, Open Future Coalition, Mighty Networks, and others). The findings are directionally consistent with Cohort 1: open-source self-hostable platforms score 85+ and pass all gates; movement-aligned hosted platforms score 70–80 with G4 concerns from US jurisdiction; commercial SaaS platforms cluster in the 40–60 range with multiple gate failures.

The decision support below is intended for boards, steering committees, and staff evaluating CRM migrations where operational fitness and commitments to data sovereignty, BDS-style procurement screens, or technology-sovereignty policies are both in scope.

Use Cases Held Constant Across Tools
What the assessment assumes the organization needs

The platforms are scored for fit against the operational needs that recur across community-based agricultural organizations. Scoring assumes membership in the low hundreds to low thousands; significantly larger or smaller scales may shift some category weights.

U1 · Segmentation

Tag and filter by region, commodity, role

A regional coordinator can pull a list of members in their geography who raise a specific commodity, or hold a specific role, without going through head office.

U2 · Mass communication

Email + open/click tracking + segmentation

Newsletter and campaign emails sent to filtered subsets, with open and click tracking sufficient to plan follow-up engagement.

U3 · Events & trainings

Webinar and event attendance recorded against member profile

Registration pages, attendance capture, post-event follow-up. Members' engagement histories accumulate into their profile.

U4 · Multi-user

Role-based access for regional coordinators

Many staff, organizers, and regional volunteers logging in concurrently with permissions that scope what each sees. No per-seat licensing tax.

U5 · Migration

Clean data import from incumbent system

CSV export from the incumbent, mature import on the destination, and a documented path that does not lose contact history, donation history, or event participation.

U6 · Multilingual

Interface and member-facing pages in multiple languages

For organizations operating in multiple official languages or across linguistic communities. UI translation, not just data fields.

U7 · Campaign tracking

Participation tracking across multi-step campaigns

Capacity to run a campaign that touches members across email, events, and direct asks, and to track which subset engaged with each component.

U8 · Data sovereignty

Hosting that aligns with org's procurement policies

For organizations with BDS resolutions, technology-sovereignty commitments, or jurisdictional preferences: a credible answer to where member data physically resides and under whose laws it is governed.

Honest Risks
What boards should also hear

The recommendation is sound but not free of trade-offs. Decisions are stronger when these are acknowledged up front rather than discovered in implementation.

  1. The right vote is often "defer and scope," not "switch now"

    An organization facing a near-term board decision between two commercial platforms can usually justify a 30–60 day deferral to obtain a formal scoping proposal from at least one open-source implementation partner. If that proposal does not come back within 20% of the commercial three-year total cost, or cannot deliver the operational needs identified, the commercial path remains available. The deferral is the procedurally defensible ask.

  2. Co-op implementation partner capacity is not guaranteed

    The worker cooperatives that make this stack work are small and oversubscribed. A scoping conversation needs to happen early in the evaluation window, not late. Have a fallback partner identified before the primary conversation begins.

  3. The open-source interface is not as polished as enterprise SaaS

    This is the honest trade-off. CiviCRM works; CiviCRM is reliable; CiviCRM is purpose-built for member organizations. CiviCRM also visibly looks like 2015. Budget for staff training and identify a CRM champion who can absorb day-to-day questions. The aesthetic gap is real but operationally crossable.

  4. "Free software" is not "free to operate"

    The numbers above include implementation and hosting because those are real costs. Boards who read "open source" and assume "no cost" will be confused by the numbers. The framing to use: free of licensing fees and vendor lock-in, paid for in operational support that funds a cooperative provider rather than a shareholder.

  5. The organization's existing CMS shapes the integration story

    CiviCRM is happiest when it lives alongside WordPress or Drupal. If the main website is on something else (Squarespace, Wix, Webflow, Ghost), integration is less seamless and some on-site member workflows will require additional planning. Worth confirming what the current website runs on before the implementation conversation.

  6. Cohort 2 is Tier 1 confidence — analyst research, not peer-reviewed

    The scores here are research-based judgment, not Tier 2 peer review. A scoping conversation with any of the named implementation partners will surface specifics that could shift category scores by ±5 points. Treat these scores as directionally accurate. Submit independent reviews via the registry's scorecard tool to progress this cohort toward Tier 2.

Framework Reference
Methodology & how to read these scores
ETA v1.0 · Four Gates + Four Categories · 100 points

Continuous with Cohort 1. The scoring rubric, gate structure, category definitions, and confidence-tier scheme are unchanged from Cohort 1's community-infrastructure assessment. The categorical findings replicate: open-source self-hostable platforms cluster at 85+, movement-aligned hosted platforms at 70–80 with G4 reservations, commercial SaaS platforms in the 40–60 range with multiple gate failures.

Entry Gates (40 points, binary pass/fail). A platform must clear basic transparency, user rights and control, freedom from abuse, and responsible stewardship before scored evaluation matters. Failing a gate caps the verdict at Conditional even if scored categories are high. G4 Safety is the gate that most often fails for this cohort: it fails when hosting on US cloud infrastructure exposes member data to the CLOUD Act and to commercial relationships an organization may have explicit procurement screens against.

Scored Categories (60 points, 0–5 per question, 15 points max each). Data Ethics covers consent, privacy, and data minimization. Interoperability covers APIs, standards, portability, and migration paths. Commons Fit covers licensing, governance, contribution model, and ownership structure. Accessibility covers languages, free tier, pricing equity, and onboarding friction.

Verdicts. ETA Eligible (≥70 pts, all gates pass) — fits the commons. Conditional (50–69 pts, or any gate fails) — may be acceptable with documented compensating controls and explicit board acknowledgment of the gate failure. ETA Ineligible (<50 pts, or multiple severe gate fails) — should not be adopted by commons-aligned organizations.

Confidence Tiers. ★☆☆ Tier 1 = analyst research / vendor claims · ★★☆ Tier 2 = peer-reviewed, community-tested · ★★★ Tier 3 = verified by CGO steward. This cohort is Tier 1 and would benefit from peer review by implementation partners and adopting organizations before final decisions.

ETA scope. ETA scores measure ethical fit, not operational fit. EspoCRM and SuiteCRM score high on ETA but are general-purpose CRMs that require significant customization to handle membership, advocacy, and event workflows. CiviCRM is the recommendation because it scores high on ETA and is purpose-built for the operational use case.
